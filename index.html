Intro

Hi! My name is Camryn Phillips, and I'm a fifth year graduate student in the Department of Astrophysical Sciences at Princeton University advised by Michael A. Strauss. I use the James Webb Space Telescope to look at galaxies in the early universe, specifically those with black holes at their centers which have material actively falling onto them and are putting out a lot of energy as a result (AGN). I try to determine if, how, and why such galaxies are interestingly different from galaxies that are not hosting AGN.

Outside of work, I knit, cross-stitch, paint, draw, play video games, play and run Dungeons and Dragons campaigns, design crossword puzzles, do crossword puzzles, play soccer, basketball, and softball, watch trivia and game shows, hike, kayak, paddleboard, downhill ski, and most importantly of all, I go birdwatching.

I do not rock climb.

Work

Spatially Resolved Spectroscopy of AGN Host Galaxies at z = 6 in the NIRSpec Fixed Slit

At high redshift, observing the host galaxies of quasars is incredibly difficult — so much so that, before the James Webb Space Telescope, such observations were impossible. Now, using JWST, we are able to investigate not only the photometry but the spectroscopy of the host galaxies of quasars using spatio-spectral analysis techniques. Over the past three years, I've worked to characterize the NIRSpec Fixed Slit PSF and apply it to quasars in order to reveal the spatially resolved spectra of their host galaxies. In my paper, I describe the characterization of the PSF, its application to 12 SHELLQs quasars, and the spatial and spectroscopic properties of the host galaxies it reveals.
